## 3. Messaging pillars

### Pillar 1: Utility bills are complicated, and mistakes happen
Commercial utility bills are built from rate schedules, tariffs, riders, demand charges, meter multipliers, power factor adjustments, fuel adjustments, taxes, franchise fees and surcharges. Utility companies bill thousands of accounts, and errors and wrong rate assignments happen. State utility regulators even have formal rules for how utilities must correct billing errors and refund overcharges. Most businesses never check line by line, because they don't have the time or the expertise.

### Pillar 2: Experience that knows where to look
AUR has been reviewing utility bills since 1986. Our auditors know the complex billing procedures used by utility companies, and they know where overcharges and hidden charges tend to hide.

### Pillar 3: Independent, and on your side
AUR is a team of independent auditors. We work for you, reviewing your bills against the applicable tariffs and riders to make sure you pay only what you owe.

### Pillar 4: No upfront cost, and you stay in control
- It costs nothing to request a review.
- AUR works on a shared-savings basis: we're paid a share of refunds and savings only after you receive them. If we don't find refunds or savings, you owe nothing.
- You approve any action before anything changes.

### Pillar 5: Minimal time and effort
Request a review online in under a minute, upload a recent month of bills, and sign one authorization. AUR does the rest, including requesting your billing history directly from the utilities.

### Pillar 6: Money back, and savings going forward
AUR looks for two kinds of value: **refunds** for past overcharges and billing errors, and **ongoing savings** from the right rate schedule and corrected charges.

## 4. What AUR looks for (proof of expertise)

Use these specifics; they show real expertise and make content credible:

- **Wrong rate schedule**: the business is on a rate that doesn't fit how it actually uses energy.
- **Demand charge and demand reading errors**: overstated peak-demand readings can inflate bills.
- **Meter errors and multipliers**: incorrect meter readings or multiplier settings.
- **Power factor charges** that may not be correct.
- **Fuel adjustments, riders and surcharges** applied incorrectly.
- **Taxes and franchise fees** charged when an exemption applies or at the wrong rate.
- **Sewer charges on water that never reaches the sewer**: many cities offer sewer credits for water that evaporates (cooling towers, irrigation) or goes into products, usually measured by a separate deduct meter.
- **Estimated bills** that were never corrected with actual readings.
- **Duplicate or unexplained charges.**

## 5. Industry angles

**Restaurants and hotels:** "Kitchens, HVAC, water heating, laundry: your utility bills are some of your biggest monthly costs. Are they right?" Restaurants use roughly 5–7 times more energy per square foot than offices and retail stores (ENERGY STAR). Angles: demand charges, rate schedules, water/sewer charges.

**Manufacturers and processors:** "Your utility bills are complex. So is the tax on them." Angles: industrial rate schedules, demand charges, power factor. In Missouri, electricity, gas and water used in manufacturing or processing can be exempt from state sales tax (RSMo 144.054), and overpaid sales tax can generally be claimed back within three years (RSMo 144.190). Other states have similar exemptions. AUR's past results include sales-tax savings for manufacturers.

**Schools and universities:** "Every dollar recovered from a utility overcharge is a dollar back for students." K-12 schools spend more than $8 billion a year on energy, their second-largest expense after personnel (ENERGY STAR). Angles: many accounts and buildings; no upfront cost; independent review.

**Hospitals and healthcare:** "Round-the-clock operations mean big, complex utility bills." Healthcare energy costs run about $10 billion a year (ENERGY STAR). Angles: demand charges, rate schedules, sewer charges.

**Churches and nonprofits:** "Put utility refunds back into your mission." Angles: no upfront cost; we handle the paperwork; the review requires little time from staff or volunteers.

**Cities and counties:** "An independent check on every utility account the public pays for." Angles: many accounts; accountability; shared savings. (Public entities follow their own procurement rules; AUR can provide what they need.)

**Agriculture:** "Irrigation, grain handling, livestock: know your rates are right." Angles: irrigation water and sewer credits, seasonal demand, rate schedules, and tax exemptions where they apply.

**Oil and gas, and pipelines:** "High-load operations deserve high-accuracy billing." Angles: industrial rates, demand, power factor. AUR's past results include large savings for a gas plant, a pipeline company and an oil and gas company.

## 9. Guardrails (every piece of content)

**Always:**

- Include a clear call to action: "Request your free bill review at aur.neuclix.com."
- Say "no upfront cost" or "free to request a review" accurately: requesting and reviewing is free; AUR's fee is a share of refunds and savings actually received.
- Label dollar results as individual examples, and add "Results vary."
- Keep claims about AUR to what's in this guide.

**Never:**

- Promise or guarantee savings or refunds ("you will save", "guaranteed refund").
- Say "most businesses are overcharged" or quote an error-rate statistic such as "80% of bills contain errors." These figures are widely repeated but have no reliable source.
- Invent numbers, percentages, testimonials or case studies.
- Name AUR clients or use their logos without written permission.
- Say AUR is licensed, government-approved or endorsed by a utility or regulator, or that AUR represents clients before a utility commission.
- Use scare tactics or pretend to be the utility company.
