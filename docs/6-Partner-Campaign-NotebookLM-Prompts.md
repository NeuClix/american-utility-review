# Partner Campaign – NotebookLM Prompts

Use these prompts in the AUR NotebookLM notebook to create the direct marketing pieces referral partners share with businesses:

1. a short video
2. a blog post
3. social media posts
4. LinkedIn posts

Everything you make shows up on every partner's dashboard, already connected to that partner's own link, QR code and flyer.

---

## 1. How it fits together

1. **Make it in NotebookLM** with the prompts below.
2. **Review it** against the checklist in section 7.
3. **Put the finished files in Google Drive › AUR Partner Campaign**, or paste the text into a chat with Claude.
4. **It's published to the website** (aur.neuclix.com) and appears on every partner's dashboard:

    - **Video and images** go in the **Marketing kit** with **Download** and **Copy caption** buttons. The caption already contains the partner's personal link.
    - **The blog post** becomes a page on aur.neuclix.com. Each partner gets a **Copy my link** button with their code attached, so anyone who reads the article and then requests a review is credited to them.
    - **Social and LinkedIn posts** go in **Share-ready posts**, filled in with the partner's link. Partners copy, paste and attach the video or image.

5. Partners keep their **personal flyer, QR code and share buttons**. The new pieces point back to the same tracking link.

### Why the link isn't in the video or images
A video or image is the same file for every partner, so it can't carry one partner's link. It ends with "aur.neuclix.com". The partner's personal link goes in the **caption** or post text, and the dashboard fills that in for each partner automatically.

## 3. Video

**Where:** Studio › **Video Overview** › click the pencil (Customize).
**Settings:** Format **Brief** for a short social video, or **Explainer** for a 3–5 minute version to send by email; Language English.
**Paste this into "What should the AI hosts focus on?":**

> Make a short video for owners and managers of businesses and organizations (restaurants, hotels, manufacturers, schools, hospitals, churches, farms) titled "Could your business be owed a utility refund?"
> Open with the hook: "When was the last time someone checked your utility bills line by line?"
> Explain that commercial electric, gas and water/sewer bills are built from rate schedules, demand charges, meter multipliers, riders, taxes and fees, and that billing errors and wrong rate assignments happen.
> Name three things American Utility Review's auditors check: the rate schedule, demand readings, and sewer charges on water that never reaches the sewer.
> Say AUR has reviewed utility bills since 1986, is independent, and has no upfront cost: AUR is paid a share of refunds and savings only after the client receives them.
> Show how easy it is: request in under a minute, upload a recent month of bills, sign one authorization, and nothing changes without the client's approval.
> You may use one result as an example, exactly as "For example, AUR found a $100,000 refund for a health club due to a demand reading error," followed by "Results vary; every situation is different."
> End with: "Request your free bill review at aur.neuclix.com."
> Rules: use only facts from the sources; never promise or guarantee savings or refunds; don't say most businesses are overcharged or quote any error-rate statistic; don't name any clients; don't say AUR is licensed, endorsed or government-approved.

**After it's made:** download the video (⋮ › Download). NotebookLM videos are landscape. For Instagram Reels or TikTok, a vertical version can be made from it later.

**Caption for partners** (goes on the dashboard with the video):

> Could your business be owed a utility refund? American Utility Review has audited commercial electric, gas and water bills since 1986. It's free to request a review, with no upfront cost: {link}
> (I'm an AUR referral partner and may earn a referral fee.)

## 4. Blog post

**Where:** Studio › **Reports** › **Blog Post** (or **Create your own**).
**Paste this as the description or custom prompt:**

> Write a blog post of 700–900 words titled "Could your business be owed a utility refund?" for owners, CFOs and facility managers of businesses and organizations.
> Structure:
> (1) a short opening hook about how rarely anyone checks commercial utility bills line by line;
> (2) "Why commercial utility bills go wrong": rate schedules, demand charges, meter multipliers, power factor, riders, taxes and fees, estimated bills, and the fact that state utility regulators have formal rules for correcting billing errors and refunding overcharges;
> (3) "Where refunds and savings hide": 5–6 short sections using the specifics in the Marketing Guide (wrong rate schedule, demand reading errors, meter errors, sewer credits for water that doesn't reach the sewer, taxes charged when an exemption applies, including Missouri's sales-tax exemption for utilities used in manufacturing, RSMo 144.054);
> (4) "What a review looks like": request, upload a recent month of bills, sign one authorization, auditors review, findings shared, nothing changes without your approval;
> (5) "What it costs": nothing upfront; AUR is paid a share of refunds and savings only after the client receives them;
> (6) two or three example results, each introduced as an example and followed by "Results vary; every situation is different";
> (7) a closing call to action: "Request your free bill review at aur.neuclix.com."
> Use plain, confident language and short paragraphs with subheadings. Format in Markdown.
> Rules: use only facts from the sources; never promise or guarantee savings or refunds; no error-rate statistics; don't name clients; cite ENERGY STAR if you use its sector figures.

**After it's made:** copy the whole report text and send it to Claude, or save it to the Drive folder. It gets published at aur.neuclix.com/blog/… with a request button, and added to partner dashboards.

## 5. Social media posts

**Where:** the notebook **chat**, or Studio › Reports › **Create your own**.
**Paste this:**

> Write social media posts that American Utility Review's referral partners will share with business owners they know. Write in the first person, as the partner (a friend or colleague, not the company).
> Write:
> - 3 Facebook posts (40–90 words each)
> - 3 Instagram captions (40–80 words each, with 5–8 hashtags)
> - 4 X posts (under 230 characters each, including the link)
> Use different angles: the hook "When was the last time someone checked your utility bills line by line?"; restaurants and hotels; manufacturers and sales tax on utilities; schools, churches and nonprofits; and no upfront cost.
> Put the exact text {link} wherever the link goes (on Instagram write "link in my bio" instead).
> End every post with "(I may earn a referral fee.)" or "#ad", except Instagram, which ends with "Partner referral: I may earn a fee."
> Label each post like "FACEBOOK – 1", "INSTAGRAM – 2", "X – 3".
> Rules: use only facts from the sources; never promise or guarantee savings or refunds; no error-rate statistics; don't name clients; if you use a dollar result, say it's one example and add "Results vary."

## 6. LinkedIn

**Where:** the notebook **chat**, or Studio › Reports › **Create your own**.
**Paste this:**

> Write LinkedIn content for American Utility Review's referral partners to share with business owners, CFOs, controllers and facility managers in their network. Write in the first person, as the partner, in a professional, helpful tone.
> Write:
> - 3 LinkedIn posts (120–220 words each), each with a strong first line, short paragraphs and 3–4 hashtags. Angles: (a) why commercial utility bills deserve a line-by-line review; (b) one example result from the sources, labeled as an example with "Results vary"; (c) for CFOs and controllers: a documented audit, refunds recovered and no upfront cost.
> - 1 short LinkedIn article (400–600 words) titled "The line items on your utility bill nobody checks."
> Put the exact text {link} where the link goes. End every post and the article with: "Full disclosure: I'm an American Utility Review referral partner and may earn a referral fee."
> Label each one like "LINKEDIN – 1" and "LINKEDIN ARTICLE".
> Rules: use only facts from the sources; never promise or guarantee savings or refunds; no error-rate statistics; don't name clients; don't say AUR is licensed or endorsed.

**Tip:** partners get the best reach on LinkedIn by posting the **video** natively (uploaded, not linked) with the post text, and putting their link in the post.

## 7. Review checklist (before anything goes to partners)

- ☐ No promised or guaranteed savings ("you will save", "guaranteed refund").
- ☐ No "most businesses are overcharged", "80% of bills", or any other invented statistic.
- ☐ Every dollar result is labeled as an example and followed by "Results vary."
- ☐ No client names or logos.
- ☐ No "licensed", "government-approved" or "endorsed by the utility".
- ☐ "Free" is accurate: free to request a review, no upfront cost, AUR paid a share of refunds and savings received.
- ☐ Every post ends with the referral-fee disclosure.
- ☐ Posts use `{link}` where the link goes (the dashboard swaps in each partner's link).
- ☐ Video and images end with aur.neuclix.com.
- ☐ Facts and pronunciations in the video are right (NotebookLM hosts sometimes improvise; regenerate if they do).
